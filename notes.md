#1 Dynamic Resize — legitimate gap, good fix. A storage filesystem with no resize story is a real omission, not a nitpick. The segmented 1MB checksum table is the correct fix — standard extensible-array pattern. One hole in their proposal though: the "Master Checksum Index Table" that tracks segment pointers has the exact same contiguous-growth problem they just solved for the checksum table itself. They didn't say how the index table avoids becoming the new bottleneck. Push back on that before merging it in.
#2 Capacity monitoring — legitimate, low-risk to add. Silent ENOSPC on the metadata drive is genuinely bad — that's the drive you said yourself is "more valuable than data." 80/90/95% thresholds are boilerplate ops practice, nothing controversial. Should have been in v0.2, mild miss, not a big one.
#3 Compression clarification — not a gap, just an FAQ answer. Nobody serious was expecting bit-level RLE under a fixed-4KB-block model; the reasoning here is just restating what your own architecture already implies. Fine to add as a footnote, not something you were "sloppy" to omit.
#4 SSD sizing wording — trivial. A find-and-replace, not architecture.
#5 Bootstrap auto-update security — this is the sharpest catch in the set. Auto-fetching and running a binary on an arbitrary untrusted host without signature verification is a real, exploitable supply-chain hole, and it's the kind of thing that's easy to miss when you're deep in filesystem semantics and thinking about drives, not adversaries. Worth taking seriously. But their fix is incomplete too: pinned public key + A/B slots handles binary integrity, but doesn't say what happens when that key needs to rotate (compromise, algorithm deprecation). A hardcoded pinned key with no rotation path is itself a long-term liability. Flag that back at them.
